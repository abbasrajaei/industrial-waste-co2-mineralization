# XRF and XRD characterisation of BOF slag

The raw basic oxygen furnace (BOF) slag was characterised before acid extraction and CO₂ mineralisation. X-ray fluorescence (XRF) was used to determine the bulk chemical composition, while X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to examine the crystalline phases present in the raw material.

## XRF characterisation

The supplied XRF dataset reports a high calcium content together with a considerable iron content. The principal components were 51.79 wt% CaO, 18.65 wt% Fe, 11.30 wt% SiO₂, 3.97 wt% MgO, and 3.79 wt% MnO. The reported free-CaO content was 14.90 wt%.

| Component | Average (wt%) | 1σ (wt%) |
|---|---:|---:|
| CaO | 51.79 | 3.80 |
| Fe | 18.65 | 3.50 |
| SiO₂ | 11.30 | 2.30 |
| MgO | 3.97 | 2.00 |
| MnO | 3.79 | 0.70 |
| Free CaO | 14.90 | 5.40 |

Free CaO is a subset of the total CaO value and must not be added to it. The high CaO content shows that the slag contains a large calcium inventory that could be used for CO₂ mineralisation. The free-CaO fraction is particularly relevant to acid extraction because free CaO is generally more readily dissolved than calcium incorporated into less reactive mineral phases.

The high iron content is also important for indirect mineralisation. Iron can dissolve together with calcium during acid extraction; the resulting leachate therefore requires purification before carbonation when a calcium-rich solution is needed for CaCO₃ precipitation.

## XRD characterisation

The XRD pattern of the raw BOF slag contains numerous sharp reflections against a relatively low background, indicating that the sample is predominantly crystalline.

Several closely spaced peaks occur between approximately 31° and 38° 2θ, with further reflections between about 40° and 64° 2θ. When considered together with the XRF composition and commonly reported BOF-slag mineralogy, these reflections are consistent with the possible presence of:

- Tricalcium silicate, Ca₃SiO₅
- Srebrodolskite, Ca₂Fe₂O₅
- Periclase, MgO
- Free lime, CaO

These assignments are tentative because the supplied XRD file contains the measured pattern and peak positions, but no phase-matching or Rietveld-refinement output.

Strong reflections are also present at approximately 11.81°, 20.89°, 23.55°, 29.28°, and 31.27° 2θ. This set of reflections may be associated with a calcium sulfate phase, potentially gypsum. The slag was analysed before extraction or carbonation, so any such phase was already present in the industrial material. Its exact identity and origin cannot be established from the diffraction pattern alone.

## Relevance to CO₂ mineralisation

The XRF and XRD results explain why this BOF slag was selected as a calcium source for indirect CO₂ mineralisation. XRF showed a total CaO content of 51.79 wt%, including 14.90 wt% free CaO. The diffraction pattern is also consistent with calcium occurring in several crystalline forms, potentially including free lime and calcium-silicate phases.

The characterisation therefore identifies two important features of the raw BOF slag: a high calcium inventory that can supply calcium for carbonation, and a high iron content that makes leachate purification necessary before CaCO₃ precipitation.
