# XRF and XRD characterisation of cement kiln bypass dust

The raw cement kiln bypass dust (CKD) was characterised before acid extraction and CO₂ mineralisation. X-ray fluorescence (XRF) was used to determine the bulk chemical composition, while X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to identify the crystalline phases present in the raw material.

## XRF characterisation

Calcium was the dominant component, with a CaO-equivalent content of 51.59 wt%. This was considerably higher than the contents of SiO₂ (14.74 wt%), Al₂O₃ (4.38 wt%), Fe₂O₃ (2.13 wt%), and MgO (0.46 wt%).

| Component | Content (wt%) |
|---|---:|
| CaO | 51.59 |
| SiO₂ | 14.74 |
| Al₂O₃ | 4.38 |
| Fe₂O₃ | 2.13 |
| MgO | 0.46 |

The high CaO content indicates that the material contains a large calcium inventory that may be recovered and used to form stable calcium carbonate.

## XRD characterisation

In the XRD pattern, the horizontal axis represents the diffraction angle, 2θ, and the vertical axis shows the measured intensity in counts. Each peak is associated with diffraction from one or more crystalline phases. Phase identification and quantitative values were obtained by comparing the measured pattern with reference crystal structures. Because some peaks overlap, the phase percentages should be interpreted as quantitative XRD estimates rather than exact bulk-composition measurements.

Calcite was the most abundant identified crystalline phase, representing 29.9 wt% of the sample. Its presence is mainly related to the CaCO₃-containing limestone or raw-feed material used in cement production. Therefore, the calcite detected in the raw sample should not be considered a product of the CO₂ mineralisation experiment.

The sample also contained the clinker phases larnite, hatrurite, brownmillerite, and C₃A. Larnite and hatrurite together represented 22.6 wt% of the sample. These phases show that fine clinker particles were carried with the dust and collected through the kiln bypass system.

Free lime and portlandite accounted for 7.7 and 2.9 wt%, respectively. Free lime can remain when calcium oxide has not fully reacted with silica and other components during clinker formation.

Sylvite and halite show the presence of potassium and sodium chlorides. Arcanite, syngenite, anhydrite, and gypsum represent potassium- and calcium-containing sulfate phases. These chloride and sulfate compounds are expected in bypass dust because volatile salts circulate within the kiln system and condense in cooler parts of the process. The bypass system removes part of this material to prevent excessive accumulation of chlorides, alkalis, and sulfates in the kiln.

Quartz represents residual silica from the cement raw materials. The smaller amounts of gehlenite and akermanite are calcium-containing silicate and aluminosilicate phases associated with the raw feed and high-temperature kiln reactions.

## Relevance to CO₂ mineralisation

The XRF and XRD results explain why this cement kiln bypass dust was selected as a calcium source for indirect CO₂ mineralisation. XRF showed that calcium was the dominant component, while XRD confirmed that calcium occurred in several mineral forms.

Free lime and portlandite are expected to provide more readily reactive calcium. Calcium is also present in larnite, hatrurite, brownmillerite, gehlenite, akermanite, and sulfate phases. The existing calcite content must be distinguished from CaCO₃ produced during the subsequent mineralisation experiment.
