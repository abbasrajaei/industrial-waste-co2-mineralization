# Phase III: Mineral Carbonation of Purified BOF Slag Leachate

## Objective

The final stage converted dissolved calcium in the purified basic oxygen furnace (BOF) slag leachate into solid calcium carbonate. A carbonate solution was produced by capturing CO₂ in 2 M NaOH and was subsequently reacted with the Ca-rich solution recovered from the pH-swing purification stage.

This route combines CO₂ capture and mineralisation. CO₂ is first converted into aqueous carbonate and then stored as stable CaCO₃.

Only selected BOF results obtained through the HCl route are reported because of confidentiality restrictions.

## Process sequence

The complete BOF treatment route consisted of:

> **BOF slag → HCl extraction → pH-swing impurity removal → purified Ca-rich solution → carbonate addition → CaCO₃ precipitation**

During the preceding pH-swing stage, Al, Si, Fe, Mn, and Mg were progressively removed. The remaining Ca-rich leachate was retained for mineral carbonation.

## Preparation of the carbonate solution

CO₂ was captured using 2 M NaOH. The reaction converted dissolved NaOH into sodium carbonate:

> **2NaOH(aq) + CO₂(g) → Na₂CO₃(aq) + H₂O(l)**

Two moles of NaOH are required to capture one mole of CO₂. The CO₂-loaded solution therefore contained approximately 1 M Na₂CO₃.

The carbonate concentration was calculated from the initial NaOH concentration:

> **C(Na₂CO₃) = C(NaOH) ÷ 2**

> **C(Na₂CO₃) = 2.0 ÷ 2 = 1.0 mol L⁻¹**

## Calcium carbonate precipitation

The HCl-derived purified leachate contained dissolved Ca²⁺, with Cl⁻ as the principal counter-ion. Addition of Na₂CO₃ caused CaCO₃ to precipitate. The molecular reaction can be represented as:

> **CaCl₂(aq) + Na₂CO₃(aq) → CaCO₃(s) + 2NaCl(aq)**

The corresponding net ionic reaction is:

> **Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → CaCO₃(s)**

The general divalent-metal carbonation reaction is:

> **M²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → MCO₃(s)**

where M²⁺ represents a divalent metal ion capable of forming an insoluble carbonate.

The molar ratio of Ca²⁺ to CO₃²⁻ is 1:1. One mole of dissolved calcium therefore requires one mole of carbonate and produces one mole of CaCO₃.

## Experimental mineralisation basis

One litre of purified BOF solution was used as the calculation basis. The solution contained 8.973 g L⁻¹ dissolved Ca.

The amount of dissolved calcium was calculated as:

> **n(Ca²⁺) = m(Ca) ÷ M(Ca)**

> **n(Ca²⁺) = 8.973 ÷ 40.078 = 0.2239 mol**

The stoichiometric carbonate requirement was therefore:

> **n(CO₃²⁻) = n(Ca²⁺) = 0.2239 mol**

For a 1 M Na₂CO₃ solution:

> **V(Na₂CO₃) = n(Na₂CO₃) ÷ C(Na₂CO₃)**

> **V(Na₂CO₃) = 0.2239 ÷ 1.0 = 0.2239 L = 223.9 mL**

A 5% carbonate excess was applied to promote more complete Ca precipitation:

> **V(Na₂CO₃, supplied) = 223.9 × 1.05 = 235.1 mL**

Therefore, 235.1 mL of the CO₂-derived 1 M Na₂CO₃ solution was reacted with 1 L of purified BOF leachate.

## CaCO₃ production

The maximum theoretical CaCO₃ mass was calculated from the dissolved calcium inventory:

> **m(CaCO₃, theoretical) = n(Ca²⁺) × M(CaCO₃)**

> **m(CaCO₃, theoretical) = 0.2239 × 100.086 = 22.41 g**

At a Ca precipitation efficiency of 98%:

> **m(CaCO₃, formed) = 22.41 × 0.98 = 21.96 g**

Solid-liquid separation, material transfer, and drying gave a solid recovery of 90%:

> **m(CaCO₃, recovered) = 21.96 × 0.90 = 19.76 g**

The recovered dry precipitate weighed 20.80 g and contained approximately 95% CaCO₃:

> **m(CaCO₃ in product) = 20.80 × 0.95 = 19.76 g**

The overall conversion of dissolved Ca into recovered CaCO₃ was:

> **Overall recovery (%) = [m(CaCO₃, recovered) ÷ m(CaCO₃, theoretical)] × 100**

> **Overall recovery = (19.76 ÷ 22.41) × 100 = 88.2%**

Approximately 0.179 g of Ca remained dissolved after carbonation. Based on the combined liquid volume after carbonate addition, the predicted residual Ca concentration was approximately 145 mg L⁻¹.

## CO₂ uptake calculation

The mass fraction of CO₂ in pure CaCO₃ was determined from the molecular masses of CO₂ and CaCO₃:

> **w(CO₂ in CaCO₃) = M(CO₂) ÷ M(CaCO₃)**

> **w(CO₂ in CaCO₃) = 44.01 ÷ 100.086 = 0.4397**

The mass of CO₂ fixed in the recovered CaCO₃ was calculated as:

> **m(CO₂ stored) = m(CaCO₃) × [M(CO₂) ÷ M(CaCO₃)]**

For a product containing less than 100% CaCO₃, the general equation is:

> **m(CO₂ stored) = m(product) × w(CaCO₃) × [44.01 ÷ 100.086]**

where:

- **m(product)** is the recovered dry-precipitate mass
- **w(CaCO₃)** is the CaCO₃ mass fraction in the recovered product

For the BOF-derived product:

> **m(CO₂ stored) = 20.80 × 0.95 × (44.01 ÷ 100.086)**

> **m(CO₂ stored) = 8.69 g CO₂**

## CO₂ storage capacity

The original extraction used 10 g of BOF slag with 150 mL of solution. Therefore, 1 L of purified solution corresponded to 66.67 g of treated BOF slag:

> **m(slag equivalent) = 10 × (1,000 ÷ 150) = 66.67 g**

The general CO₂ storage-capacity equation is:

> **CO₂ storage capacity (kg CO₂ t⁻¹ slag) = [m(CO₂ stored) ÷ m(slag)] × 1,000**

The CO₂ and slag masses must be expressed in the same unit. The equivalent calculation based on the recovered product is:

> **CO₂ storage capacity = {m(product) × w(CaCO₃) × [44.01 ÷ 100.086] ÷ m(slag)} × 1,000**

For the BOF mineralisation process:

> **CO₂ storage capacity = (8.69 ÷ 66.67) × 1,000 = 130.4 kg CO₂ t⁻¹ BOF slag**

Before solid-recovery losses, the CaCO₃ formed corresponded to a mineralisation capacity of 144.9 kg CO₂ t⁻¹ BOF slag. The capacity based on the recovered product was 130.4 kg CO₂ t⁻¹ BOF slag.

## Main findings

- Carbonate produced by capturing CO₂ in 2 M NaOH precipitated dissolved Ca²⁺ from the purified BOF leachate as CaCO₃.
- One litre of purified BOF leachate yielded 20.80 g of dry precipitate containing 19.76 g of CaCO₃.
- The recovered product corresponded to an overall Ca-to-CaCO₃ recovery of 88.2% and a CO₂ storage capacity of 130.4 kg CO₂ t⁻¹ BOF slag.
